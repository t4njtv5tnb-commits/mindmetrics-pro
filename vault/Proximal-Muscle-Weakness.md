# Proximal Muscle Weakness

> **TL;DR** — Proximal + symmetric + no numbness = think **muscle**. First move: CK, TSH, vitamin D, drug chart.

**Proximal** = muscles closest to the trunk (shoulders, hips, thighs, upper arms).
Classic story: trouble **rising from a chair**, climbing stairs, lifting arms to comb hair — while grip and fine finger movements stay normal.

---

## Why Proximal First?

```
FUEL / TISSUE problem  → big hungry muscles fail → PROXIMAL weakness (muscle)
WIRING problem         → longest wires fail      → DISTAL weakness (nerve)
```

> Analogy: *In a drought, the biggest crops wilt first. Proximal muscles are the big crops.*

---

## The 4-Part Chain

Remove any one → weakness.

| # | Part | Role | Disease example |
|---|------|------|----------------|
| 1 | Motor neuron | The wire | MND, radiculopathy |
| 2 | Neuromuscular junction | The plug | Myasthenia, Lambert-Eaton |
| 3 | Muscle | The meat | **Myopathy** ← most proximal cases |
| 4 | Fuel / environment | Power supply | Thyroid, electrolytes, vitamin D |

> **Proximal symmetric weakness without numbness → points hardest at #3 (myopathy)**

## MED TIME — Full Differential

| Letter | Bucket | Key Examples |
|--------|--------|-------------|
| **M** | Medication / Toxin | **Statins** ← #1 reversible cause; steroids, fibrates, colchicine, alcohol |
| **E** | Endocrine | **Hypothyroid** (up to 80%), thyrotoxicosis, Cushing's, hyperparathyroid |
| **D** | Deficiency | **Vitamin D** / osteomalacia, hypokalaemia, hypocalcaemia, hypophosphataemia |
| **T** | Toxic-Inflammatory | Dermatomyositis, polymyositis, IMNM (very high CK), antisynthetase, IBM |
| **I** | Inherited | Muscular dystrophy (Duchenne, LGMD), myotonic dystrophy type 2, Pompe, mito |
| **M** | Motor junction | **Myasthenia gravis**, **Lambert-Eaton** — the impostors |
| **E** | Emergency | Rhabdomyolysis, GBS, hypokalaemic periodic paralysis |

### 3 routing questions
```
Q1  Drug chart?              → M (commonest reversible)
Q2  Endocrine / electrolytes? → E + D
Q3  Fatigable or rash?       → Junction impostors / Inflammatory
    Lifelong + family hx?    → Inherited
```

## CK — The Key Rule

> **CK rules myopathy IN, never OUT.**

- **High CK** → confirms muscle damage
- **Normal CK** → does NOT rule out myopathy

Why normal CK ≠ no myopathy:
1. **Dermatomyositis** attacks blood vessels (perifascicular), not fibres directly
2. **Chronic / late disease** → muscle replaced by fat/scar → less to leak
3. Quiet phase or on steroids
4. IBM classically has normal or near-normal CK

*Analogy: CK is a smoke alarm that only triggers on flames. Some fires are slow smoulders — real damage, no alarm.*

If CK normal but still suspicious: **aldolase + myositis antibody panel**

---

## Investigation Ladder

### Tier 1 — Always first (every patient)
- Drug chart review (statins, steroids, fibrates, colchicine, amiodarone, alcohol)
- **CK** (creatine kinase)
- **TSH**
- **Vitamin D** (± Ca, PO₄)
- **Electrolytes** (Na, K, Ca, Mg)
- FBC, LFTs, glucose / HbA1c, ESR / CRP

### Tier 2 — High CK or still unexplained
- Myositis antibody panel (anti-Jo1, anti-HMGCR, anti-SRP)
- Aldolase (raised when CK normal — dermatomyositis)
- ANA, RF — connective tissue overlap
- Malignancy screen (selected inflammatory myopathy cases)

### Tier 3 — Specialist / secondary care
- **EMG + NCS** — separates muscle vs nerve vs junction
- **Muscle MRI** — shows oedema, guides biopsy site
- **Muscle biopsy** — tissue diagnosis
- **Genetic testing** — young onset, family history

### 🚨 Refer Urgently If
- Respiratory muscle weakness (breathlessness lying flat)
- Swallowing difficulty (aspiration risk)
- Cardiac involvement
- Rapidly progressive weakness
- Very high CK + dark urine → **rhabdomyolysis → AKI risk**

## The Junction Impostors

Both give pure-motor proximal weakness, no sensory loss — identical to myopathy. Key: **fatigability**.

| Feature | Myasthenia Gravis | Lambert-Eaton (LEMS) |
|---------|------------------|---------------------|
| Fatigability | Worse at day's end | Briefly improves post-exercise |
| Key signs | Ocular ptosis, bulbar | Dry mouth, autonomic, reflexes return |
| Association | Thymoma | **Small-cell lung cancer** |
| Discriminator | Repetitive stimulation (decrement) | Repetitive stimulation (increment) |

---

## Muscular Dystrophy

**4 defining features:**
1. Genetic — mutation in a gene coding for a structural muscle protein
2. Protein — e.g. dystrophin (the mortar between muscle fibres and their frame)
3. Degeneration — progressive fibre death
4. Replacement — fat + fibrosis fills the gap

**CK sky-high even before obvious weakness** (leak starts at birth once fibres contract).

*Analogy: Dystrophin = mortar in a brick wall. No mortar → bricks loosen with every contraction → repair crew (satellite cells) tires → gaps fill with rubble.*

### Classic presentations
- **Boys** — Duchenne: Gowers' sign, calf pseudohypertrophy, CK ~9000+
- **Adults** — Myotonic dystrophy type 2: grip won't release, cataracts, family history
